A cold-open proposal · For the City & the Recreation & Park District

New revenue for our parks. No new taxes.

Pleasant Hill's parks serve 100,000+ people a year across 269 acres. After Measure S, the money to renew them can't come from residents. So let it come from the businesses that want to reach them. This is a partnership that pays the District first — and never asks the taxpayer for a dime.

The opportunity · Why Pleasant Hill, why now

The support is there.
The funding path isn't.

In November 2024, 63.5% of Pleasant Hill voters said yes to a $77M parks bond. It still failed — two-thirds was the bar. The will of the community is not the problem. The mechanism is.

Private capital at risk. Not the General Fund.

Two bond measures. Two near-misses. Measure A in 2020, Measure S in 2024 — a community that clearly loves its parks, twice unable to clear the two-thirds bar to tax itself for them.

Meanwhile the City is projecting structural General Fund deficits through the five-year forecast. The message from both is the same: the next dollar for Pleasant Hill's parks will not come from a tax. It has to come from somewhere new. That "somewhere" is a market the District already owns — and has never sold.

The model · How it works

The District has the audience.
Local business has the budget.
Nobody has built the bridge.

Every park, field, scoreboard, program and event is a place a family gathers — and a place a local business wants to be seen. Kinetic Culture builds and runs that marketplace, and hands the District the majority of every dollar.

01

We package the assets

Fields, scoreboards, events, digital reach, naming opportunities — inventoried and priced like the media assets they are.

02

We sell them

Kinetic Culture brings the sales capability the District has never had — and the relationships with brands that want local families.

03

Revenue is shared

The District receives the majority share of every agreement — new, recurring, non-tax revenue with no upfront cost.

04

The community wins

Pools get renovated. Fields get lights. Programs get funded. Residents pay nothing — and local businesses come home.

Aligned incentives, by design.

The structure is deliberately simple, because simple is what survives a public meeting. A revenue-share partnership: the District keeps the majority of every dollar, Kinetic Culture earns its share only after revenue is generated. We don't get paid unless you do.

Through the economic-development lens

New revenue, activated assets, zero General Fund exposure.

You spent 14 years in economic development before this desk — you already think in terms of activating underused assets and structuring public-private deals. This is that, applied to parks: private capital at risk, not the General Fund, generating recurring revenue while the City faces structural deficits. No staff to hire, no line item to defend. It's the kind of deal you'd have chased in Walnut Creek — now landing in your own city.

"What would you do with a new revenue stream that costs the taxpayer nothing?"
Through the operator's lens

Turnkey execution. Minimal lift on your staff.

You've spent a career getting things built and run well — and you know your team is already stretched. This is designed so Kinetic Culture carries the sales, the fulfillment and the partner management, while the District keeps full approval over every placement. Nothing gets sold that you and the board don't sign off on. The upside is revenue that funds the projects you actually want; the operational cost to your staff is close to zero.

"A partner who does the work — and makes your facilities better."
Through the chair's lens

The facilities voters wanted — without the bond.

You chaired this board through a funding gap the community couldn't close at the ballot box. Measure S wasn't rejected on the merits — it was rejected by a supermajority threshold, with 63.5% in favor. This model is the alternative path to the same outcome: a way to fund the pool renovation, the field improvements and the capital projects residents clearly want — with private dollars instead of a property-tax increase.

"A funding path that doesn't depend on two-thirds of the ballot."
Through the governance & legal lens

A clean contract, airtight risk transfer.

You advise boards on exactly these questions — fiduciary duty, liability, how to finance improvements without exposing the organization. So this is built to survive your scrutiny: a written revenue-share agreement, indemnification and insurance carried by the partnership, category and placement approval reserved to the District, and no financial obligation on the District's side. "Zero taxpayer risk" isn't a slogan here — it's a contract term your review would hold us to.

"If the agreement is bulletproof, this protects the District — and funds it."
Through the youth-sports lens

More money where the kids actually play.

You coach — you've stood on these fields. You know what a set of new lights, a renovated cage, or a funded scholarship program means to a family. The single largest revenue stream in Year 1 flows from youth sports: tournaments, field sponsorships and the Pleasant Hill Baseball Association's home at Pleasant Oaks. This is built to put dollars directly back into the programs and fields you serve — funded by businesses that want to support local kids.

"Better fields, more programming — paid for by the community, for the community."
