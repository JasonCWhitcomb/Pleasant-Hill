The big idea

What if our parks became something the whole community owns?

Not sold. Not plastered in billboards. Owned — by the local businesses that want to invest where they live, and the neighbors who want to leave something behind. Common Ground turns Pleasant Hill's most-loved places into a self-sustaining engine that funds their own future. Everyone puts in a little. Everyone shares in what it builds.

Local business wins

Roots, not billboards. A genuine, visible place in the community they serve — and marketing dollars that finally stay in Pleasant Hill instead of leaving it.

Neighbors win

A share, a bench, a name on the field your kids play on. A real way to leave a mark on the place that raised you — for the next family that shows up.

Pleasant Hill wins

New, recurring funding for pools, fields and programs — the projects the community wants — without asking a single resident to pay another cent in taxes.

Why now

This community already loves these places. It's just never been asked to invest in them — the right way.

When residents were asked to fund a $77M parks bond, 63% said yes. It didn't clear the two-thirds bar — but the message was unmistakable: Pleasant Hill deeply cares about its parks. The appetite isn't in question.

What's missing is a way to fund these places that doesn't depend on a supermajority — or a single taxpayer dollar. Meanwhile, the Rec & Park District's most valuable assets sit in front of 100,000 people a year, quietly unmonetized. The demand is already here. The bridge to it isn't built yet.

How it works

Not just a program to run — a strategy to own.

No upfront cost. No new staff. No General Fund exposure. Kinetic Culture builds and runs it — but the real value is the strategy behind it: pricing the right assets, courting the right partners, and compounding a few good relationships into outsized, lasting outcomes. The District keeps control and keeps most of every dollar.

Package

We inventory the assets — fields, scoreboards, events, digital reach, naming, resident shares — and price them like the real opportunities they are.

Invite

We bring the partners — the local businesses that want roots here, and the families who want a piece of it. Everyone gets a way in, at every level.

Reinvest

The money comes home. Most of every dollar flows straight back into Pleasant Hill's parks and programs — the projects this community actually wants.

Compound

Partnerships renew and grow each year with demand. Managed with real strategy, a single good season becomes a permanent, compounding source of community funding.

Where the money goes

Most of every dollar stays right here.

For every dollar raised, the lion's share goes straight back into the District's parks and programs. The remainder funds the team that builds and runs the strategy — paid only after the money is raised, never before. No cost to the District. No cost to the taxpayer.

Through the operator's lens

Turnkey to run. Minimal lift on your team.

You've spent a career getting things built and run well — and your staff is already stretched. Common Ground is designed so Kinetic Culture carries the sales, fulfillment and partner management, while the District keeps full approval over every placement. The upside is funding for the projects you actually want; the cost to your team is close to zero.

"A partner who does the work — and leaves your facilities better."
Through the chair's lens

The projects the community wants — funded a new way.

You've led this Board while a community that clearly loves its parks looked for a way to fund their future. This is that way: a path to the pool, the fields and the capital projects residents want — built with community and business dollars instead of a property-tax increase, and without waiting on a two-thirds vote.

"A funding path the whole Board can stand behind."
Through the governance & legal lens

A clean agreement, airtight risk transfer.

You advise boards on exactly these questions — fiduciary duty, liability, funding improvements without exposure. So this is built to survive your review: a written revenue-share agreement, insurance and indemnification carried by the partnership, category and placement approval reserved to the District, and no financial obligation on the District's side. "No taxpayer risk" is a contract term here, not a slogan.

"Structured to protect the District — and fund it."
Through the youth-sports lens

More where the kids actually play.

You coach — you've stood on these fields. You know what new lights, a renovated cage, or a funded scholarship means to a family. This is built to put real dollars back into the programs and fields you serve, funded by businesses that want to support local kids.

"Better fields and more programming — for the community, by the community."
Through the contracts & community lens

A professional deal, with real community benefit.

"New revenue the whole Board gets to direct."
